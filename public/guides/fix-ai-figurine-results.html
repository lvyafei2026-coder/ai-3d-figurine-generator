AI Figurine Looks Wrong? How to Improve Face, Framing and Details

A generated figure can look polished but still miss the details that matter. Use this troubleshooting guide to identify what to change before trying again.

Home / Guides / AI Figurine Looks Wrong? How to Improve Face, Framing and Details

AI figurine generation is not perfectly predictable. A small change to the reference photo can affect the face, outfit, pose and composition. Instead of changing everything at once, identify the visible problem and adjust one input at a time.

Problem → likely cause → what to try

What looks wrongPossible causeWhat to try next
The face does not look recognizableFace is small, blurred, shadowed or partly coveredUse a sharper, front-facing photo with even light and no sunglasses.
The body or outfit looks inventedThe source image is a tight face cropTry a half-body or full-body photo with the clothing visible.
The figure is hard to seeBackground competes with the subjectChoose the Studio scene for a cleaner product-style composition.
The style feels inconsistentSeveral visual ideas compete in one conceptPick one style direction, such as chibi, anime or realistic.
Small details look distortedFine objects and tiny text are difficult for image modelsUse fewer accessories and avoid relying on tiny printed words.

Fix likeness first

When the face is the main issue, do not start by changing the background. Choose a photo where the face is in focus and occupies a useful part of the frame. Remove filters and avoid extreme camera angles. If you have another photo of the same person with clearer lighting, compare the two inputs.

Fix framing second

If the result crops the body or invents clothing, the source may not show enough of the person. A half-body or full-body image gives the model more visual information. For a classic action figure, the full silhouette and visible feet help the collectible read as a complete object.

Fix the scene third

Use a Studio scene when you want a neutral product image. Choose Shelf or Gaming when the environment is part of the concept. Keep the scene simple during troubleshooting so you can judge the figure itself.

Understand what the generator can and cannot control

Toolara uses preset styles and scenes rather than a manual prompt editor. That makes the workflow simple, but it also means you cannot directly type a correction for a particular finger, accessory or package label. The practical controls are the source photo, style preset and scene choice. Review the output and decide whether another source photo is worth trying.

Be realistic about retries: Free daily generation capacity is limited. Avoid repeatedly submitting the same photo without changing anything. Make a deliberate adjustment first.

A simple retry workflow

  1. Save the first result. Keep the original output so you can compare changes instead of relying on memory.
  2. Name the main problem. Decide whether the issue is likeness, outfit, crop, style or background. Avoid trying to fix five things at once.
  3. Change the input that relates to the problem. For weak likeness, change the photo. For a missing outfit, use a wider crop. For a busy scene, choose a simpler setting.
  4. Keep the other choices stable. Do not change the style and scene while testing a new photo unless one of those is the problem.
  5. Compare at the same size. Review the face and silhouette at full size, then check the overall composition at thumbnail size.

How to tell whether another attempt is worthwhile

A retry is most useful when you can state what will be different. A sharper portrait is a meaningful change; uploading the same image again with no adjustment may simply produce another variation without teaching you anything. Because generation capacity can be limited, make a deliberate change before using another attempt.

When the issue is not the image

Sometimes the output is technically clear but does not match the idea you had in mind. Revisit the style choice: a realistic figure and a chibi figure communicate very different proportions. If the figure is fine but the presentation feels wrong, change the scene rather than replacing the photo.

Also distinguish visual quality from file expectations. Toolara produces a 2D PNG image. It does not provide a rigged character, editable 3D mesh or printer-ready STL/OBJ file. If you need a physical object, the next steps involve a separate 3D modeling workflow.

Quick review before downloading

  • Is the face recognizable enough for your intended use?
  • Does the figure have a complete and readable silhouette?
  • Are the outfit and accessories consistent with the concept?
  • Does the background support rather than compete with the figure?
  • Is there any distorted text or accidental logo that should not be shared?

If the answer to the most important questions is yes, the image may be ready to download. If not, choose one specific correction and decide whether a new input photo or a different preset is the more sensible next step.

When the output is good enough

Check the face, overall silhouette and any visible text at full size before sharing. If the image is for a gift or public post, make sure you have permission to use the source person's likeness. For a physical object, remember that a figurine-style PNG is not itself a 3D-printable model; follow the separate 3D printing guide for the next steps.
